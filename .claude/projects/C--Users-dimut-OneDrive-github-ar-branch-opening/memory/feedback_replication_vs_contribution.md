---
name: Don't dwell on replications of prior work; lead with the new thing
description: When a result in the paper replicates a well-established finding from prior work, acknowledge in one paragraph and use it as the puzzle/setup for the paper's actual contribution. Don't structure the section as if the replication were the headline.
type: feedback
---

If a result the paper reports is a replication of a finding already established in cited prior work (e.g., NRS 2026 showing that bank branch entry concentrates in high-deposit-beta markets), do not give it a headline subsection. The reader has already read the prior paper or will recognize the result from the cite. Giving it equal billing with the paper's contribution dilutes the contribution and signals that the author has not internalized what is new versus what is recycled.

**Treatment pattern:**
1. Open the results section with a one-paragraph framing that names the replicated finding, attributes it to the prior paper, and turns it into the *puzzle* the paper is going to resolve. Format: "Prior work (cite) shows X. We confirm X in our larger sample. But X creates a puzzle: if Y, why would profit-maximizing agents do X? The rest of this section conjectures that Z and tests it."
2. Report the replication coefficient briefly with a reference to the table, in one paragraph, no separate subsection. Do not re-derive the economic magnitude in detail; the prior paper did that.
3. Devote the subsections to the contribution. The interaction tests, the mechanism splits, the heterogeneity that the prior paper did not study --- these get the structural weight.
4. Close the section by reading the contribution back against the puzzle: "The puzzle posed at the top of the section --- why open in markets where the average deposit dollar is less profitable --- resolves once we condition on Z. Banks open in those markets precisely where Z is true."

**Why:** User feedback on first-draft-may2026 results section: "Banks Open in More Contestable Markets is the same result from narayanan2025decline. Don't duplicate it. Setup as a puzzle: why do banks open in high-beta areas, conjecture that there must be some other advantage that allows them to monetize." A subsection structure that mirrored "deposit beta result / interaction result / discussion" gave the replicated finding the same architectural weight as the contribution; the correct structure treats the replication as setup and the channel interactions as the substance.

**How to apply:** Before drafting any results section, list every finding the section will report and tag each one as either *replication* or *contribution*. Replications go in the opening framing paragraph or in a single transitional paragraph; subsection headings and the bulk of the prose go to contributions. A paper that has $n$ contributions should not have more than $n+1$ result subsections.
