\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \adddescription{
    \small
This figure plots the annual count of de novo branch openings in the United States from 2012 onward, stacked by the opening bank's total-asset bucket at the time of opening. The vertical axis reports the number of openings; the horizontal axis is calendar year. An opening is the first appearance of a branch identifier (UNINUMBR) in the FDIC Summary of Deposits; institution-level transfers from the Office of Thrift Supervision to the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency in 2011 are excluded. Asset buckets are defined on the opening bank's reported SOD assets in the year of opening: $<$\$1B, \$1B--\$10B, \$10B--\$100B, and $\geq$\$100B. The four colors correspond to these four buckets in the legend, ordered from smallest at the bottom of the stack to largest at the top.
    }
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\textwidth]{figures/fig_openings_by_size_stacked.png}
    \caption{De Novo Branch Openings by Bank Size}
    \label{fig:openings_by_size_stacked}
\end{figure}

This table reports summary statistics for the bank--zip--year panel used in the branch-opening regressions, separately by bank-asset bucket. Panel A covers banks with assets above \$100B and reports the dependent variable, the key right-hand-side variables, and the full set of zip-level controls used in the regressions. Panels B and C cover banks with assets in \$10B--\$100B and \$1B--\$10B, respectively, and report only the dependent variable and key right-hand-side variables (the controls have the same definitions and similar moments and are therefore omitted to save space). The dependent variable, $opening$, equals one if bank $b$ first appears as a branch in zip $z$ in year $t$. The key right-hand-side variables are: the bank--cycle deposit beta, an indicator for a positive rate-paying advantage relative to incumbents, an indicator for an interest-expense-to-assets undercut share above 0.5, and indicators for the bank's mortgage and CRA small-business presence in the zip at $t-1$. The sample is restricted to non-missing values of the baseline controls and lags, and the variables ($\beta$, rate-paying advantage, and the zip-level growth and demographic controls) are winsorized within year at the 1st and 99th percentiles. Mean, standard deviation, the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentiles, and the number of non-missing observations are reported.
